# Faster, lower-energy, memory-constrained inference for mixture-of-experts (MoE) LLMs: research notes (state as of 21 November 2026)

Reading guide

- Tags on each finding: **[A]** model-agnostic and training-free (works on any released MoE checkpoint, exact output unless stated); **[A-cal]** agnostic but needs an offline calibration / profiling pass per model; **[P]** needs a small predictor trained per model (base weights untouched); **[FT]** needs fine-tuning of the router, adapters or the model; **[ARCH]** needs a specific architecture or training from scratch.
- "Background" marks work older than 2025.
- "Abstract-level" means only the abstract or a search snippet was read; numbers are the authors' own and were not checked against the full paper.
- Almost all numbers below are self-reported by the authors. Baselines differ between papers, so speedups are not comparable across papers.

## 1. Expert offloading, caching and prefetching: how systems predict, which cache policies, what hit rates and speedups

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the field has split into two camps: exact systems (true router output always executed; a predictor only hides load latency) which report 5-80% decode gains from prefetch plus 20-40% from better eviction, and approximate systems (predicted, substituted or cache-biased experts are executed) which report 1.5-4.5x but pay a model-dependent quality cost. Next-layer prediction is now routinely 85-97% accurate with cheap predictors, but next-token (temporal) prediction is weak on modern many-expert models, and the best published SSD-backed results on 16-24 GB machines (Edge0 at 20 tok/s for a 35B model in 3 GiB; CECAR at 12.5 tok/s for Qwen3-30B-A3B on a 16 GB GPU) both change the routing rather than only predicting it.

### Cited Findings

Prediction method: reuse the model's own router one layer early (training-free)

- Fate (Feb 2025, rev. May 2025): feeds layer i's gate input into layer i+1's gate on the CPU. Raw accuracy 78.79% in decode; restricting transfers to experts above the 75th-percentile confidence gives 97.15%. A "shallow-favoring" cache (all experts of layers 0-3 resident, ARC eviction elsewhere) gives 99.08% hit rate. Up to 4.5x prefill and 3.7x (body) / 4.1x (abstract) decode over load-on-demand; quality change within about 1 point on a 24 GB card. Models DeepSeekMoE-16B and Qwen1.5-MoE; RTX 3090 and 1080 Ti, experts in host RAM. **[A-cal]** (offline profiling sets the layer boundary and the INT2 share) — [Fate](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12224v2)
- HOBBIT (Nov 2024, background): current layer's gating input predicts experts for the next 1-3 layers; top-1 next-layer accuracy averages 96%, about 90% for two or three layers ahead. On a miss it loads a low-precision copy instead when the expert's gate weight is small (Mixtral: 67% high precision, 30% low precision, 3% skipped), with at most 1% accuracy drop on GSM8K / TruthfulQA. Cache priority mixes LRU, LFU, high-precision-frequency and layer distance, cutting miss penalty 4.69-8.68% vs LRU and 2.13-4.19% vs LFU. Up to 9.93x decode vs MoE-Infinity on Jetson AGX Orin; on RTX 4090 + CPU only 0.99-1.46x vs Fiddler. Mixtral-8x7B and Phi-MoE only. **[A]** but needs two precisions of every expert on disk — [HOBBIT](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.01433v2)
- "Speculating Experts" (Mar 2026, Univ. of Maryland / Together AI): parameter-free. Offline it stores one "default vector" per expert (its mean output); at layer l it forms a quasi-hidden state (post-attention residual + gate-weighted default vectors, then next layer's norm) and runs the next layer's router on it. Recall@k about 90% beyond the early layers on Qwen3-30B-A3B. TPOT falls 9-14% on an A6000 and 5-8% on A100 / GH200 versus on-demand CPU-to-GPU loading (batch 1). Important: the paper executes the speculated experts instead of re-fetching. That keeps gpt-oss accuracy (gpt-oss-20b 0.779 vs 0.799 baseline; gpt-oss-120b 0.857 vs 0.849) but badly hurts Qwen3-30B-A3B (average 0.817 to 0.641; GSM8K 0.950 to 0.576). A small trained estimator on low-hit layers recovers Qwen3 to 0.766 (88% hit on gpt-oss-20b, 83% on gpt-oss-120b, about 90% on Qwen3 after 4-5M training tokens). **[A-cal]** for the predictor; executing speculated experts is model-sensitive — [Speculating Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19289v1)
- DALI (Feb 2026): offline inter-layer residual vectors correct the gate input ("residual-based prefetching", no training). Prefetch accuracy 79.5% (DeepSeek-V2-Lite) and 93.3% (Qwen3-30B-A3B) versus 72.6% / 77.6% for HybriMoE. A workload-aware cache replacement using a sliding window of recent per-expert token counts gives about 38% gain (1.23x over HybriMoE's score-based replacement); prefetching itself only about 9%. Decode 3.97x vs llama.cpp, 2.16x vs KTransformers, 1.32x vs HybriMoE on RTX 3090 + 64-core EPYC. HybriMoE's hit rate on Mixtral was measured at only 25.3%. **[A-cal]** — [DALI](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03495)
- ST-MoE (June 2026, simulator study): a cross-layer correlation table (per expert, top-K correlated experts in the next layer with 2-bit confidence) plus a history table of the previous token's experts; 85% overall prediction accuracy; mispredictions are fetched on demand so routing is unchanged. Models Qwen1.5 / Qwen2 MoE, DeepSeek-V2, DeepSeekMoE. Hardware numbers are from a cycle-level accelerator simulator, not a real device. **[A-cal]** — [ST-MoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15453)

Prediction method: small learned predictor per layer (base model frozen)

- PreScope (Sept 2025): "LLaPor" per-layer predictor with different architectures for input / middle / output layer groups, trained offline on 128 batches of 512 tokens for about 30 epochs. Top-4 hit rate 94% (DeepSeek-V2-Lite), 99% (Mixtral), 97% (Qwen3-30B-A3B), 99% (Moonlight-16B-A3B); 15-68.4% better than gate-reuse predictors. Cross-layer scheduling with a cost model and async transfers that split each expert into three sub-matrices on separate streams. Throughput +141% vs Klotski, +70.8% vs HybriMoE on V100 / A100 with experts in host RAM. Removing the hot-expert table cut cache hit rates 30-40%. **[P]** — [PreScope](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23638v1)
- Pre-attention expert prediction (Nov 2025, ETH): two linear functions on the activations before the attention block of the same layer, trained with a ranking-aware loss; also covers the first layer. Accuracy 93.03% (DeepSeek-V2-Lite), 94.69% (Qwen3-30B), 97.62% (Phi-mini-MoE), about 15 points above prior predictors. Abstract-level. **[P]** — [Pre-attention prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10676v1)
- PROBE (Feb 2026, Kuaishou; data-center expert parallelism): predictor = frozen copy of the next layer's router plus a trainable residual MLP, distilled online. Per-layer top-K accuracy 87-94% after distillation versus 70-80% for the untrained router copy; "top-half-K" hit rate near 100%. Models Qwen3-235B and gpt-oss-120b. **[P]** — [PROBE](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00509v2)
- OD-MoE (Dec 2025): a quantized "shadow" copy of the whole model runs ahead and its routing is used as the prediction; recall 99.94% (FP16 shadow), 97.34% (INT8), 95.67% (NF4), versus AdapMoE 86%, DAOP 84%, HOBBIT 91% as re-measured by these authors. Cacheless: each expert is loaded just in time and evicted. Mixtral-8x7B on a ten-node RTX 3090 testbed, 3.69 tok/s (75% of fully cached). Needs enough memory and compute to run a second model. **[A]** — [OD-MoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03927v1)

Prediction method: predict the next token's routing and use it as the routing (model modified)

- Edge0, "The Other Half of the Memory Wall" (Sept 2026): Apple-silicon MLX engine. A per-layer "prerouter" head (hidden state + one-hots of the current and previous token's top-k experts; fc-GELU-fc, width 512, warm-started from the next layer's router) predicts routing one token ahead, and the prediction is the routing, so nothing prefetched is wasted. A recovery LoRA (not merged) compensates for int4 quantization plus the routing replacement. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (40 layers, 256 experts per layer): 20.4 tok/s decode at 2.9 GiB peak active memory on a 24 GB Mac mini M4 Pro, with K reduced to 4 active experts; vanilla resident mlx-lm on the same machine: 3.9 tok/s at 18.2 GiB. On a 16 GB MacBook M2 the prerouter roughly doubles decode over on-demand streaming: 4.8 to 8.6 tok/s (K=2), 3.5 to 6.4 (K=4), 1.8 to 3.3 (K=8). On the 24 GB machine disabling the prerouter only drops 20.4 to 19.9 tok/s (disk reads are at most 12% of a step there). Quality: average 79.2 vs 83.2 for the fp16 teacher over five benchmarks (AIME 2026 86.6 vs 92.7; HumanEval 90.9 vs 95.1; GPQA-Diamond 79.8 vs 81.8; MMLU-Pro 81.0 vs 84.6; IFBench 57.9 vs 61.7). Training: head distillation, SFT on about 2M teacher rows, on-policy distillation on about 200k rows. **[FT]** — [Edge0 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18063), [Edge0 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18063v2)
- Pre-gated MoE (2024, background) and Read-ME (NeurIPS 2024, background): routing decided one layer ahead (pre-gate, needs full fine-tune) or by a single router decoupled from the backbone (Read-ME; about 1B tokens of extra training; up to 6.1% lower mean end-to-end latency). **[ARCH]/[FT]** — [Read-ME](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19123), [summary of pre-gated MoE in Speculating Experts related work](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19289v1)

Cache replacement policies and measured hit rates

- FlashMoE (Jan 2026, KAIST): SSD-backed, experts stored as separate files, per-layer expert cache in VRAM. A small MLP (3 layers, hidden 128, about 113 KB per layer) scores eviction candidates from recency and frequency features and is trained to imitate Belady's oracle on routing traces. Hit rate +21% vs LRU and +51% vs LFU on OLMoE-1B-7B; up to +28% vs ARC and +21% vs LeCaR. In the motivating example Belady reaches about 86% and LRU about 73%. Token throughput over LRU: about +22% (OLMoE), +7% (Qwen3-30B-A3B). Under LRU, 34.2% of evicted experts are needed again within 5 steps (0.1% under Belady). Expert load about 3 ms each from a PCIe 5.0 NVMe (7.4 GB/s rated); expert FFN compute 158 microseconds. Up to 2.6x over Fiddler / DAOP / llama.cpp. Desktop with RTX 5070 Ti 16 GB. **[P]** (cache policy only; whether it transfers across models or datasets is not stated) — [FlashMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17063v1)
- CECAR (NeurIPS 2026 poster, same group): argues hit rates are bounded even under Belady and that the bound is too low for low-latency use, and that binary "is it cached" routing bias causes instability; instead an ML policy predicts each expert's expected reuse distance and routing favours experts with short predicted distance. 12.5 tok/s for Qwen3-30B-A3B on a 16 GB consumer GPU with non-resident experts on SSD; 4.5x over conventional MoE inference, and faster in decode than baselines holding the full model in DRAM. Abstract-level; no accuracy numbers on the page. **[P]**, routing is altered — [CECAR](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/152287)
- "Paging the Experts" (Sept 2026): Swift/MLX runtime ("Routide") for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on iPhone 17 Pro Max. Replay of five 128-token traces: at a 512 MiB expert budget LRU and FIFO get 0.00% hits while random eviction gets 18.80%; at 576 MiB LRU 38.58%, FIFO 25.66%; at 1,024 MiB LRU 47.59%, recency-frequency hybrid 49.83%; offline farthest-next-use with pinning 54.49% / 56.93% / 68.23%. Cause of the cliff: the shortest observed reuse distance is 312 distinct experts (one forward touches 40 layers x 8 = 320 experts) and only 303 payloads fit in 512 MiB, so LRU always evicts just before reuse. A simple prefetch (previous step's top expert in the same layer if weight >= 0.20, prefill only) was right 61.31% of the time; about 60% of 10,334 speculative loads were useful; a variant that refreshed recency on lookups lost hits. Decode 1.80-3.17 tok/s on the phone. **[A]** — [Paging the Experts abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29032), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29032v1)
- Tideway (10 Nov 2026, Duke): treats VRAM + host RAM as one pool with a residency table (cross-tier duplicates reclaimed lazily, VRAM victims written back to RAM only when no RAM copy exists), phase-aware layouts (prefill: anchored reusable experts plus a small streaming window for SSD-backed weights; decode: fixed-address expert slots and RAM as LRU), parallel SSD reads on multi-miss, and piecewise CUDA graphs split at the router. Observations: an 8K-token prefill chunk activates nearly the whole expert pool, so LRU under a cyclic layer scan evicts exactly what the next chunk needs (baselines pull 86-99% of prefill expert traffic from SSD although RAM holds 44% of the pool; Tideway 52%); llama.cpp spends 92% of decode time computing experts on the CPU. Average 1.97x end-to-end, 2.40x TTFT, 1.61x decode throughput (range 1.20-2.28x) versus the fastest of FreeToken, llama.cpp, and re-implemented SSD-LLaMA and FlashMoE. Models Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Gemma4-26B-A4B (BF16), DeepSeek-V4-Flash FP4 (155 GiB); RTX 5090 32 GB, host RAM capped 16-128 GiB, SN850X SSD measured 6.8 GB/s. No lookahead prediction used. **[A]**, exact — [Tideway abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14848), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14848v1)
- MoE-CORE (Oct 2026): per-layer cache capacities that differ by layer, domain-informed cache initialization, routing-history-aware replacement, cross-layer prefetch; main configuration executes router-selected experts exactly, with optional score-based substitution for low-score misses. Mean TPOT 38.0-44.8 ms on DeepSeek-V4-Flash-W4A8 and 206.6-220.5 ms on GLM-5.2-W4A8C8 versus about 1,269 ms and 5,942 ms for "vLLM Prefetch" (the authors note the two were run with different output caps). NPU appliance with an 84 GB device-memory cap. Abstract-level. **[A]** — [MoE-CORE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01950)
- llama.cpp merged GPU expert cache (PR #29887, merged Oct 2026): plain LRU over experts kept in host memory, only misses uploaded; hit rate 72-77% with a cache of about 10% of expert bytes (6.5 GiB of 65.4 GiB) and 89% with 19 GiB, giving 1.57-2.20x decode on RTX 4090 / 5090 — [llama.cpp PR #29887](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29887)
- Earlier systems (background, 2023-24), for lineage: EdgeMoE (Aug 2023; experts on storage, per-expert bit widths, predictive preloading pipeline) — [EdgeMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14352); MoE-Infinity (Jan 2024; request-level activation tracing, 2-20x / 3.1-16.7x latency improvement over offloading baselines) — [MoE-Infinity](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.14361v3); Fiddler (Feb 2024, ICLR 2025; run cold experts on the CPU rather than moving weights; 1.26x single-batch) — [Fiddler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07033); MoE-Lightning (Nov 2024; up to 10.3x throughput for Mixtral on a T4) — [MoE-Lightning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11217v1.pdf); ExpertFlow (Oct 2024; predictive caching + token scheduling, 2-10x) — [ExpertFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17954v2); SwapMoE (Aug 2023; keeps a small dynamic set of "virtual experts" in memory) — [SwapMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.15030v4). A survey notes EdgeMoE and MoE-Infinity use LFU and Mixtral-Offloading uses LRU — [Survey on MoE inference optimization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.14219)
- 2025 hybrid CPU-GPU schedulers: HybriMoE (1.33x prefill, 1.70x decode over prior hybrid frameworks) — [HybriMoE](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05897); Klotski (multi-batch pipeline, up to 85.12x throughput) — [Klotski](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06888v1). A third-party re-implementation (MatMoE authors) reports only 1.1-2.0x from HOBBIT on a Raspberry Pi 5 simulator (search-snippet level) — [MatMoE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01704)

### Inferences
- Next-layer prediction is a solved problem at the 90%+ level without training (Fate, Speculating Experts, DALI) and at 94-99% with a tiny per-layer predictor (PreScope, pre-attention). What it buys is bounded by how much of a step is load latency: 5-14% when experts sit in host RAM behind fast PCIe (Speculating Experts), about 9% in DALI, but about 80% on a 16 GB Mac where SSD reads dominate (Edge0's 16 GB A/B). For a 16 GB unified-memory laptop with the model larger than RAM, prediction is therefore worth far more than on the desktop-GPU setups most papers measure.
- One layer of lead time is short for SSD. Published expert loads are about 3 ms per expert from a PCIe 5.0 NVMe (FlashMoE) while one layer of compute on a small-expert model is well under that; this is why the two strongest SSD results (Edge0, CECAR) moved to next-token prediction or routing bias, which give a whole token of lead time. An exact engine would need multi-layer lookahead (HOBBIT's 2-3 layers at about 90%, or the "strong cross-layer representational similarity" that Speculating Experts leaves as future work) to get a comparable window without changing outputs.
- Eviction policy matters about as much as prediction: Belady-imitating or workload-aware policies give 7-38% throughput over LRU (FlashMoE, DALI), and LRU has a hard failure mode when the cache is smaller than one forward pass's worth of experts (Paging the Experts: 0% hits at 303 slots versus a 312-expert minimum reuse distance; llama.cpp disk-offload PoC requires `ubatch * n_expert_used <= n_slots`). A per-layer cache budget, rather than one global LRU, avoids this by construction.
- What a 16 GB unified-memory laptop needs to reuse these ideas: (1) the non-expert core (attention, routers, shared experts, embeddings) resident — 1.35 GB for Gemma-4 26B-A4B in TurboFieldfare, 5.5 GB for Qwen3.5-397B in flash-moe; (2) an expert slot pool sized per layer and above one forward's demand; (3) a predictor with at least one token or several layers of lead; (4) reads issued from a CPU thread while the GPU computes the non-routed part of the layer. Items 1, 2 and 4 are model-agnostic; item 3 is agnostic only in its training-free forms.
- Model-agnostic and exact: Tideway-style residency management, LRU / LFU / learned eviction, Fate / DALI / ST-MoE prefetch with on-demand fallback, MoE-CORE main configuration. Agnostic but approximate: HOBBIT low-precision-on-miss, executing speculated experts. Needs training per model: PreScope, pre-attention predictors, FlashMoE / CECAR policies (small), Edge0 (large: heads + LoRA + distillation).

### Gaps
- No paper found reports multi-layer (2+ layers ahead) prediction accuracy for the 2025-26 many-expert models (Qwen3 / Qwen3.5 / Qwen3.6, gpt-oss, DeepSeek-V3 / V4, GLM, Gemma-4 MoE); HOBBIT's 90% is for Mixtral and Phi-MoE with 8-16 experts.
- CECAR's accuracy cost and its predictor details were not available (only the NeurIPS abstract page was readable).
- Edge0 does not report a standalone prerouter recall, nor results with the native K (8 active experts) at full quality; its headline number uses K=4.
- Tideway reports only relative numbers; absolute tok/s and its behaviour at 16 GiB host RAM are not in the text read.
- No prefetch paper measures energy; all optimize latency.

## 2. Expert activation locality: reuse across consecutive tokens, popularity skew, and biasing routing toward cached experts

### Takeaway
Measured temporal locality in released MoE models is weak and family-dependent: adjacent tokens share roughly a quarter of a layer's experts on Qwen3.6-35B, gpt-oss-20b switches expert sets on more than half of tokens, and LRU caches holding half of all experts still miss 22-40% of the time on 2024 models; popularity skew is real but workload-dependent and sometimes absent (near-uniform routing reported for DeepSeek-V4-Flash). Routing can be biased toward cached experts at 0.1-3% perplexity cost when miss rates stay above about 10%, but several 2026 papers show this breaks down below 5% miss rate and that fine-tuning the router for reuse is the more reliable route.

### Cited Findings

Measured consecutive-token reuse

- Qualcomm "Mixture of Cache-Conditional Experts" (Dec 2024, rev. June 2025): states that current MoEs lack strong temporal locality. With an LRU cache holding half of each layer's experts, miss rates on WikiText are 35% (Qwen1.5-MoE, 30 of 60 cached), 28% (DeepSeek-V2-Lite, 32 of 64), 22% (Phi-3.5-MoE, 8 of 16), 40% (Mixtral, 4 of 8); mean expert cache lifetime 26, 19, 22 and 5 tokens — [Cache-Conditional Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00099v2)
- "Not All Models Suit Expert Offloading" (Fudan / Huawei, May 2025, v3 Dec 2025): 20 MoE LLMs compared with two metrics — SRP (best F1 achievable if an expert's on/off decision is frozen for a whole segment of m tokens) and SCH (oracle cache hit rate for a cache of rho times the active-expert count). SRP at m=16: LLaMA-MoE-v2 78.2, Yuan2.0 63.5, Qwen3 54.1, Phi-3.5-MoE 52.0, OLMoE 50.9, GRIN-MoE 50.4, Mixtral-8x7B 49.4, JetMoE 47.5, DeepSeek-V2-Lite 37.9, DeepSeekMoE 36.9, Qwen2 36.7, Qwen1.5-MoE 30.7. Design factors: shared experts lower local consistency; fewer experts or fewer active experts lower it; stronger load balancing lowers it (toy models: no load balance 56.4, over-balanced 36.4). Domain-specialized experts raise it (SRP on arXiv / StackExchange / GitHub text can be over 10% above global). Recommends a cache of about 2x the active-expert count as the knee for most models — [Local routing consistency](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16056v3)
- Edge0 (Sept 2026): on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, adjacent tokens agree on only about a quarter of a layer's expert set, so many prefetched-by-history experts are never reused — [Edge0](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18063v2)
- "Temporally Extended MoE" (Princeton, Apr 2026): gpt-oss-20b switches expert sets at over 50% of tokens. Abstract-level; a search snippet also attributes switch rates near 1 for gpt-oss-120b and Qwen3-Next-80B to this paper, which the abstract page did not confirm — [Temporally Extended MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20156)
- ST-MoE (June 2026): consecutive-token overlap is "nearly 2 times" the random baseline K^2/N across Qwen1.5 / Qwen2 / DeepSeek-V2 / DeepSeekMoE — [ST-MoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15453)
- flash-moe (2026, Apple M3 Max, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B with 512 experts per layer, K=4): "temporal expert prediction" had a 25% hit rate and cost 18% speed; an MLP routing predictor reached 31% accuracy; both were discarded. The OS page cache (about 35 GB) nevertheless reached about 71% hit rate — [danveloper/flash-moe](https://github.com/danveloper/flash-moe)
- llama.cpp disk-offload thread (2026), commenter measurements on Qwen3-30B-A3B (128 experts, 8 active): pinned-set hit rates — random 6%, calibrated on a 10-token pass 42%, oracle top-8 per layer 45%, calibrated + LRU 56%, oracle top-32 per layer 88% — [llama.cpp discussion #23324](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23324)
- For contrast, dense-model neuron-level reuse is much higher: NeuroPrefetcher reports 82-85% of active MLP rows are reused from the previous token (Mistral-7B) — [NeuroPrefetcher](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.22643.md)
- Prompt-length effect in prefill: the fraction of all experts touched grows sublinearly — about 47% at 32 tokens, 58% at 64, 64% at 128, 67% at 256 (FlashMoE); an 8K-token chunk touches nearly all (Tideway) — [FlashMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17063v1), [Tideway](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14848v1)

Popularity skew

- OLMoE-1B-7B: the top 32 of 64 experts capture 93% of routing weight over a 63-token draft tree; expert co-activation is 23x the uniform expectation — [MoE-Spec](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16052v1)
- DeepSeek-V2-Lite: activation frequency varies by more than 10x between experts within one MoE block (HumanEval-X) — [MxMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05799v1)
- Serving-scale imbalance ratio (max / mean expert-rank load): 1.43-2.28 during decode and above 2.6 in prefill for Qwen3-235B / gpt-oss-120b — [PROBE](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00509v2)
- Counter-evidence: a llama.cpp tester found routing near-uniform on DeepSeek-V4-Flash, where a static "pin whole layers" baseline beat a heat-map expert cache (13.45 vs 12.77 tok/s); the PR author disputed the claim. Another contributor reports a static hot/cold split gives +26% (Qwen3-Coder-Next) and +31.6% (gpt-oss-120b) and that "routing concentration determines how much a hot-list helps" — [llama.cpp PR #26563](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/26563), [llama.cpp issue #20757](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/20757)
- A PoC on gpt-oss-120b with an 8 GB GPU reports hit rate rising from 48-56% (first 80 tokens) to about 98-100% in steady state (12-14 tok/s vs 0.5-1 tok/s uncached); single-user report — [llama.cpp issue #20757](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/20757)

Biasing routing toward cached experts (training-free)

- Cache-Prior (Qualcomm): add lambda x (running mean logit range) to cached experts' router logits; only the selection changes, original gate weights are used. At lambda=0.5: miss rate 35% to 16% (Qwen1.5-MoE), 28% to 7% (DeepSeek-V2-Lite), 22% to 9% (Phi-3.5-MoE), 40% to 21% (Mixtral); perplexity up 0.1% (DeepSeek) to 2.9% (Mixtral); MMLU / GSM8K change below 0.1%. Beats the Belady bound for unmodified routing at 1% perplexity increase on all but Phi-3.5-MoE. Over 2x throughput on Snapdragon phones (12 GB, 4-bit, 30 of 60 experts cached; llama.cpp CPU, mlock); throughput rises nearly linearly with hit rate. **[A]** — [Cache-Conditional Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00099v2)
- Importance-driven expert scheduling (Aug 2025, Nanjing / Honor): substitute a low-score active expert with a cached expert whose gate score is within a threshold alpha; hit rate above 60%; TPOT -24% (batch 1) to -48% versus best baseline; GSM8K change -1.2 points (DeepSeek-MoE-16B, alpha 0.35), -0.4 (XVERSE), -0.1 (Qwen2-57B-A14B). **[A]** — [Importance-driven scheduling](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18983v2)
- BuddyMoE (Nov 2025): offline "buddy" lists from co-activation and output similarity; on a prefetch miss use a resident buddy. Up to 10% throughput with "negligible" accuracy loss on DeepSeek-V2-Lite (abstract / secondary-summary level). **[A-cal]** — [BuddyMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10054)
- Caution from MoE-CORE: "score proximity is a selection heuristic and does not establish equivalence" between original and substitute outputs (search-snippet level) — [MoE-CORE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.01950)
- SliceMoE (Dec 2025, rev. Mar 2026, KAIST): prior cache-aware routing was evaluated at 10-30% miss rates and its accuracy collapses in the below-5% regime needed for DRAM + flash; proposes caching bit-slices (see section 3) — [SliceMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12990v3)
- CECAR: binary cache-presence routing can cause routing instability; uses predicted reuse distance instead — [CECAR](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/152287)
- Speculating Experts: executing mispredicted experts is nearly free on gpt-oss but costs Qwen3-30B-A3B about 18 points on average — evidence that tolerance to substituted experts is model-specific — [Speculating Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19289v1)

Skipping, pruning, merging

- HOBBIT skips about 3% of Mixtral expert activations (lowest gate weight) within the 1% accuracy budget; AdapMoE-style dynamic skipping gives 1.36x (survey table) — [HOBBIT](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.01433v2), [Survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.14219)
- ASET (ACL 2026 Findings): training-free adaptive token-level expert skipping, at least 10% improvement on DeepSeek-V3 "without measurable loss"; the best static allocation for DeepSeek-V2-Lite does not transfer to DeepSeek-V3 (search-snippet level; PDF not readable) — [ASET](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.2140.pdf)
- REAP (Cerebras, Oct 2025, v2 Mar 2026): one-shot expert pruning by mean (gate x output norm) over active tokens. Qwen3-30B-A3B at 25% of experts removed: code 0.551 vs 0.558, math 0.888 vs 0.887, multiple-choice 0.665 vs 0.721; at 50%: code 0.541, math 0.857, multiple-choice 0.503. Qwen3-Coder-480B at 50%: SWE-Bench-Verified 0.522 vs 0.540. Merging methods lose over 5% (25%) and over 20% (50%) on coding; argued cause: a merged expert cannot follow the router's input-dependent mixing. Calibration domain matters (C4-only calibration can collapse code accuracy to 0%). **[A-cal]** — [REAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)
- Other 2026 training-free pruning: SHAPE (20% / 40% expert pruning on Qwen3-30B-A3B, gpt-oss-20b, DeepSeek-V2-Lite), AIMER, DERN, TENP (neuron-level); a router-calibration study finds fine-grained MoEs such as Qwen3-30B-A3B benefit most from router knowledge distillation after compression (search-snippet level) — [SHAPE](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09886), [Router KD study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.02217)
- A llama.cpp PoC author notes pruned ("REAP") models are not made obsolete by paging because "sparsity is not guaranteed" (DeepSeek-V4 experts were more uniform) — [llama.cpp discussion #23324](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23324)

Training the router for locality

- ReMoE (ICML 2026): router-only fine-tuning that biases toward recently selected experts; +26% expert reuse with downstream performance "maintained" on DeepSeek and Qwen models; TPOT -43.6 to -49.8% (1.77-1.99x decode) under llama.cpp on Jetson Orin NX with experts on UFS; +8.4% throughput under vLLM GPU-CPU offloading. Abstract-level. **[FT]** (router only) — [ReMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27081)
- Temporally Extended MoE (Apr 2026): per-layer controller (option-critic with a deliberation cost) trained with LoRA + self-distillation on gpt-oss-20b; switch rate from over 50% to below 5% while keeping up to 90% of base accuracy on MATH / MMLU / MMMLU. **[FT]** — [Temporally Extended MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20156)
- Sticky Routing (June 2026): a routing-consistency loss from the first pretraining step; up to 60% fewer switches at under 4% perplexity cost on small models; claims locality "is most efficiently instilled at training time". **[ARCH]** (pretraining) — [Sticky Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08780)

### Inferences
- The numbers disagree on the surface but are consistent once cache size is accounted for: with a cache of half the experts LRU hits 60-78% (Qualcomm), with about 10-30% of expert bytes it hits 72-89% (llama.cpp PR #29887), and with only one forward pass's worth it hits 0-39% (Paging the Experts). Exact-reuse between adjacent tokens (about 25% on Qwen3.6-35B; about 2x random elsewhere) is a much lower figure than the hit rate of a cache several times the active set, because popularity skew, not adjacency, supplies most hits.
- Using ST-MoE's random baseline K^2/N: for 8 of 128 experts the chance overlap is 0.5 experts per layer (6%), and for 8 of 256 it is 0.25 (3%). Edge0's "about a quarter" on a 256-expert model is therefore roughly 8x chance, yet still means about 6 of 8 experts per layer change every token — a naive "reuse last token's experts" prefetch cannot exceed about 25% useful loads there, matching flash-moe's discarded 25%-hit predictor.
- Family guidance (from Liang et al. plus the 2026 reports): Qwen3-class, OLMoE, Phi-3.5-MoE and Mixtral are comparatively cache-friendly; DeepSeek-V2-family (shared experts, strong balancing) and Qwen1.5 / Qwen2 are worse; gpt-oss switches experts on most tokens but tolerates substituted experts well; newer DeepSeek-V4-Flash may be near-uniform. No single cache policy should be assumed to transfer across families.
- Cache-biased routing is the cheapest approximate lever and is model-agnostic, but its quality cost was measured at 7-21% residual miss rates. For an SSD-backed 16 GB laptop where a miss costs milliseconds, the target is a miss rate of a few percent, which is exactly where SliceMoE and CECAR report the simple bias failing. Treat lambda-style bias as a tunable "economy mode" needing per-model quality checks, not a default.
- Expert pruning (REAP at 25%) is a static, verifiable alternative: it shrinks the on-disk pool and raises hit rate for a fixed cache at about 1-6 points cost depending on task, with no runtime machinery.

### Gaps
- No single study measures consecutive-token reuse and popularity skew with one method across Qwen3 / Qwen3.5 / Qwen3.6, DeepSeek-V3 / V4, gpt-oss, GLM and Gemma-4 MoE. Liang et al. cover Qwen3 and DeepSeek-V2-Lite but not gpt-oss, GLM or Gemma-family MoE.
- The gpt-oss-120b and Qwen3-Next switch-rate figures could not be confirmed from the abstract page.
- ReMoE's absolute reuse rates before / after and its quality table were not read (abstract only). ASET and DPA-MoE PDFs could not be parsed.
- No quality data found for cache-biased routing on reasoning-heavy benchmarks with 2026 models, other than the indirect evidence from Speculating Experts.

## 3. Batching and kernels for MoE: grouping by expert, many small experts, per-expert precision

### Takeaway
Server engines all use the same pattern (sort routed tokens by expert, pad, grouped GEMM) and most 2025-26 kernel work there targets communication for expert parallelism, which does not apply to a single GPU at batch 1; for single-request decode the kernel wins that transfer are smaller (about 1.03-1.2x reported on Apple silicon) and the larger levers are bytes moved per token: per-expert mixed precision, bit-sliced or prefix-decodable expert storage, intra-expert neuron sparsity, and native 4-bit formats such as MXFP4.

### Cited Findings

Execution pattern and kernels

- vLLM and SGLang fused-MoE paths sort routed tokens by expert and pad to the GEMM block size before running expert matmuls (search-snippet level, from the paper below) — [Intra-expert sparsity paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08575v1)
- A Rust port of mlx-lm's switch layers documents that SwitchGLU / SwitchMLP sort tokens by expert id only when routing has at least 64 index slots (search-snippet level) — [mlxrs switch.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/mlxrs/0.1.0/source/src/lm/nn/switch.rs)
- "Evaluating the Applicability of LLM Inference Optimizations on Apple Silicon" (NeurIPS 2026 workshop, Oct 2026): ported CUDA kernels transfer only partially because public Metal lacks asynchronous copy and batch-1 decode makes several serving mechanisms inapplicable; transferable parts give up to 1.20x decode. Search-snippet level details: sorting by expert pays only when many rows go to each expert (at batch-1 decode there is nothing to sort); a custom gather kernel reached about 1.05x (gate / up) and 1.23x (down) over `mx.gather_qmm`, 1.03-1.12x end to end over 66 patched layers — [Apple Silicon applicability study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31479)
- Intra-expert activation sparsity (Berkeley / AMD, May 2026): a routed token needs only a subset of an expert's neurons; zero the lowest post-activation outputs and skip them in the up and down projections (gate projection stays dense). Up to about 90% intra-expert sparsity while keeping 95% of baseline benchmark accuracy on some models; MoE-layer speedup up to 2.5x (RTX 4090, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B), 2.0x (H200), end-to-end up to 1.2x; falls back to dense at large batch. Tested on Granite-1B-A400M, OLMoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, gpt-oss-20b, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B / 122B / 397B, Llama-4-Maverick. No training, threshold table built at startup. **[A-cal]**, approximate — [Intra-expert sparsity](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08575v1)
- vLLM wide expert parallelism for DeepSeek-V3-class models (Dec 2025): DeepGEMM on by default, DeepEP all-to-all kernels, EPLB-style load balancing, dual-batch overlap, a fused silu-mul-quant kernel; combined throughput 1.5k to 2.2k tok/s per H200. Multi-node only — [vLLM large-scale serving](https://vllm.ai/blog/2025-12-17-large-scale-serving)
- Why the older tensor-parallel FusedMoE struggles with sparse many-expert models: per-expert compute becomes small and every token touches every GPU (search-snippet level) — [Red Hat: scaling DeepSeek-style MoEs](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2025/09/08/scaling-deepseek-style-moes-vllm-and-llm-d-using-wide-ep)
- SGLang 2026 releases: fused SwiGLU-up GEMM epilogue for MoE (v0.5.18), FlashInfer SM90 MXFP4 W4A8 CUTLASS MoE kernel (v0.5.19); Tencent HPC-Ops fused MoE operators cut TPOT up to 48.8% on their model; SGLang-JAX "Fused MoE V2" (scatter + expert FFN + gather in one kernel) cuts MoE prefill latency 53% (5.16 to 2.42 ms) (all search-snippet level) — [SGLang releases](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/releases), [HPC-Ops x SGLang](https://lmsys.org/blog/2026-08-07-hpc-ops-sglang)
- Batch-1 GPU utilization under CPU-offloaded experts is tiny: Mixtral decode reaches 0.76% GPU utilization on an A30; the roofline-optimal batch sizes are 824 (A30) and 515 (4090D) — [SpecMoEOff](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21706v2)
- Routing itself is cheap: under 9% of MoE-block compute for OLMoE in an instrumented llama.cpp; the dense-vs-MoE gap on edge devices comes from total-parameter memory footprint, expert dispatch overhead and KV pressure — [Does MoE Actually Help on Consumer and Edge Hardware?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21428)
- Unified-memory sync cost: in a llama.cpp Metal PoC, a CPU custom op for expert-slot remapping created a sync point that halved generation speed even with no eviction; the later design uses a tiny Metal kernel that publishes expert IDs to shared memory, a CPU sidecar thread, and an `MTLSharedEvent` the GPU waits on (38.1 tok/s vanilla vs 30 tok/s with all 128 slots, i.e. about 21% overhead for the indirection) — [llama.cpp issue #20757](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/20757), [llama.cpp discussion #23324](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23324)
- flash-moe (Metal, Qwen3.5-397B): expert compute is only about 1.2-1.3 ms of a 77-96 ms token; dense attention-side matmuls are about 54% and expert I/O 35-47%. Kept: FMA dequant kernel +12%, deferred GPU command buffers to overlap CPU work. Discarded: "speculative early routing" -38%, GPU lookup-table dequant -2%, spin-polling the GPU -23%, Metal 4 tensor matmul for single-row decode -8% — [danveloper/flash-moe](https://github.com/danveloper/flash-moe), [Anemll/flash-moe](https://github.com/Anemll/flash-moe)

Expert-level precision and storage formats

- MxMoE (ICML 2025): bit width per linear block per expert chosen by an ILP over sensitivity, activation frequency and a profiled kernel cost model, with auto-generated mixed-precision Group-GEMM kernels. At 2.25 bits: WikiText-2 perplexity 7.01 vs 8.49 for GPTQ (DeepSeek-V2-Lite), 8.79 vs 11.19 (Qwen1.5-MoE). Up to 25% more throughput than uniform W4A16 and 29.4% over W8A8; 1.6-2.7x over full precision when memory-bound. RTX 4090. **[A-cal]** — [MxMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05799v1)
- QuantMoE-Bench (2024, rev. 2025): attention needs more bits than FFN, shared experts more than routed experts, earlier MoE layers more than later ones (search-snippet level) — [QuantMoE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08155v2)
- GEMQ (ICML 2026): global linear program for expert-level bit allocation plus router fine-tuning to adapt routing to quantized experts; a snippet reports a 2.5-bit Mixtral-8x7B running on one H100 (abstract has no numbers). **[FT]** (router) — [GEMQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23078)
- MoPEQ (Sept 2025; Hessian-trace per-expert bits, vision-language MoEs) and an ICLR 2026 method using router-norm change (search-snippet level) — [MoPEQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02512), [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006533)
- SliceMoE (Dec 2025 / Mar 2026): cache experts as bit-slices — most-significant slice under LRU, least-significant slice lowest priority and fetched only for "critical" experts; a calibration-free asymmetric Matryoshka quantizer lets a high-bit expert be truncated to low-bit with no second copy; cache warm-up from prefill expert frequencies gives 2.31x lower decode energy and 1.96x speed versus an empty cache. Under a below-5% miss-rate constraint: decode energy down up to 2.37x (DeepSeek-V2-Lite) and 2.85x (Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B), speed up 1.81x / 1.64x. Evaluated with a modelled accelerator + LPDDR4 + UFS 3.1 (103 pJ/bit flash read), not a physical device. **[A]** — [SliceMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12990v3)
- MatMoE (ICCAD 2026, Nov 2026): Matryoshka PTQ so each expert can run at several precisions from one stored copy, bit width chosen per decoding step from routing scores to meet a latency target; built on llama.cpp; accuracy 4.0-18.3% above the best static mixed-precision method; 1.3-3.7x over llama.cpp on Raspberry Pi 5, 1.2-1.3x on RTX 4090 / Jetson AGX Orin. Abstract-level. **[A-cal]** — [MatMoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01704)
- DPA-MoE (EMNLP 2026 Findings): each expert stored as prefix-decodable chunks; the number of chunks read is chosen per routed activation (search-snippet level; PDF not readable) — [DPA-MoE](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1076.pdf)
- Fate: INT4 experts in cache, INT2 for the less popular 25% of experts during prefill, within 1% accuracy loss — [Fate](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12224v2)
- MXFP4: gpt-oss ships MoE weights in MXFP4 (4-bit E2M1 values in blocks of 32 with a shared scale); 20b fits in about 16 GB and 120b in about 80 GB; without MXFP4 kernels transformers falls back to bfloat16 at about 4x memory; MegaBlocks MoE kernels are not compatible with MXFP4 — [HF: tricks from gpt-oss](https://huggingface.co/blog/faster-transformers); vLLM runs gpt-oss MoE weights in MXFP4 and the rest in bf16 — [vLLM gpt-oss](https://vllm.ai/blog/2025-08-05-gpt-oss); a third-party guide says llama.cpp has first-class MXFP4 across CUDA, Vulkan, Metal and CPU and an `MXFP4_MOE` quantize type that converts only expert tensors (the same guide carries a self-correction; treat as unconfirmed) — [MXFP4 vs Q4_K_M guide](https://openclawdc.com/blog/mxfp4-vs-q4-k-m/)
- Practical mixed quantization on Apple silicon (flash-moe fork): 3-bit-class GGUF experts (IQ3_XXS gate / up, IQ4_XS down) read 23% fewer bytes than 4-bit and decode 36% faster (12.9 vs 9.5 tok/s on M5 Max) at perplexity 3.81 vs 3.64; 2-bit is fastest (14.5 tok/s) but perplexity 5.71 and it breaks JSON / tool calling — [Anemll/flash-moe](https://github.com/Anemll/flash-moe)
- TurboFieldfare (Swift / Metal, Gemma-4 26B-A4B): experts stored as 4-bit values in the exact binary layout the Metal kernels consume so loading needs no conversion; unified memory lets loaded experts be used without an extra copy (third-party write-up) — [TurboFieldfare write-up](https://betterstack.com/community/guides/ai/turbo-fieldfare.md)

### Inferences
- For 128-256 experts with 8 active at batch 1, each layer is 8 small matmuls on one row; sorting / grouping is irrelevant and kernel gains are capped near the 1.03-1.2x reported. The dominant costs become (a) bytes fetched per token and (b) CPU-GPU synchronization around the router. The Metal PoC numbers (halved speed from a sync point; about 21% overhead even with a well-designed shared-event handoff) say the router-to-slot indirection must stay on the GPU timeline, or routing must be known before the layer starts.
- Bytes-per-token levers stack and are mostly agnostic: lower-bit experts (-23% I/O for 3-bit-class vs 4-bit), slice / prefix storage so a miss can be served by a partial read (SliceMoE, MatMoE, DPA-MoE, HOBBIT), and intra-expert neuron sparsity (up to 2.5x on the MoE layer). On an SSD-backed device a low-precision-on-miss policy converts a multi-millisecond stall into a smaller read with bounded quality effect.
- "Speculative early routing" shows up as a loss (-38%) in the one Apple-silicon engine that tried it at the kernel level, while paper systems report gains from router lookahead; the difference is that on unified memory the speculative work competes with the GPU for the same memory bandwidth (see section 6).
- Formats that are stored in the layout the kernel consumes (TurboFieldfare, flash-moe packed experts, MXFP4) remove conversion work from the miss path; this is a prerequisite for per-token expert reads to be cheap.

### Gaps
- No published measurement found of a Metal / MLX grouped-expert kernel versus per-expert matmuls at batch 1 for 128-256-expert models beyond the snippet-level 1.03-1.12x; the full text of the Apple-silicon applicability study was not retrievable (HTML 404).
- No evidence found on whether MLX itself has native MXFP4 kernels as of November 2026 (community threads suggest conversion to other quantizations; unconfirmed).
- Low-rank compensation for quantized experts: no MoE-specific 2025-26 paper was found in this pass.
- Intra-expert sparsity has no reported result on Apple GPUs or at SSD-read level (it could in principle cut bytes read, but the paper only skips compute).

## 4. MoE-specific speculative decoding and early exit

### Takeaway
At batch 1, speculative decoding is structurally worse for MoE than for dense models because verifying several draft tokens activates the union of their experts — measured verification overhead is 2-3x, static speculation can slow MoE serving by up to 1.5x, and two Apple-silicon reports found it break-even or negative; the 2025-26 remedies are to gate speculation by measured utility (Cascade), cap the experts loaded per verification (MoE-Spec), or reuse the draft as an expert-demand forecast (MoE-SpAc). It turns positive when experts are already saturated (moderate batch) or when experts run on a slow device and speculation raises their per-call workload.

### Cited Findings
- Cascade (MICRO 2025, Georgia Tech / NVIDIA): verification overhead reaches 2-3x for MoEs (Mixtral: 3 draft tokens can activate up to 6 experts versus 2); static speculation length causes slowdowns up to 1.5x; worst cases 26% / 38% / 54% slowdown at K = 1 / 2 / 3. Cascade measures utility = token gain / time cost in short test phases, disables speculation when utility is below 1 and hill-climbs K: worst-case slowdown limited to 5%, throughput +7-14% over static K. Models Mixtral, Phi-3.5-MoE, OLMoE, DeepSeek-V1, Qwen1.5-MoE; RTX 6000 Ada. About 500 lines on vLLM 0.6.6. **[A]** — [Cascade](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20675v1)
- MoE-Spec (Meta, Feb 2026): on OLMoE (64 experts, 8 active) a 127-token draft tree activates 54 of 64 experts per layer; EAGLE-3 speedup peaks about 1.95x and falls to 1.85x at tree size 255. Fix: per-layer expert budget B — rank experts by summed routing probability over the tree, load only the top B, and substitute (or drop) out-of-budget experts. 10-30% more throughput than EAGLE-3 at comparable quality (Mixtral about +27%, Qwen3-30B-A3B about +16%, OLMoE about +6%); acceptance length -1.4%; Qwen3 keeps about 95% of autoregressive quality at 2.0x. An oracle ranking would need 25% fewer experts. A100, FP16, single request. Caveat stated by the authors: gains may shrink under batching. **[A]**, approximate — [MoE-Spec](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16052v1)
- MoESD (Tsinghua / Huawei, May 2025): speculative decoding helps MoE more than dense once a batch already activates all experts; peak speedup 2.29x on Qwen2-57B-A14B (HumanEval, temperature 0, gamma 4) at moderate batch sizes, on 2 x 80 GB GPUs; the paper states it is ineffective at small batch. Introduces "target efficiency" (time of one-token verify / time of gamma-token verify) as the quantity that explains the trend. **[A]** — [MoESD](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19645v2)
- SpecMoEOff (Aug 2025, rev. Oct 2025): with experts on the CPU, speculation enlarges each expert's workload; 1.45-1.53x over the same system without speculation and up to 2.5x over MoE-Lightning; Mixtral-8x7B + EAGLE draft, A30 / 4090D with 190-250 GB host RAM. **[A]** — [SpecMoEOff](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21706v2)
- MoE-SpAc (Feb-Mar 2026): uses draft-token verification as a lookahead sensor — per-expert activation counts over gamma = 8 draft tokens drive prefetch priority and eviction; hot/cold prediction accuracy about 0.85; average 25.06 tok/s versus 17.66 for llama.cpp with speculative decoding and 12.15 for HybriMoE (Qwen3-30B-A3B target, Qwen3-4B draft, RTX 4090 24 GB, batch 1); about +42% over the best speculative baseline. **[A]** — [MoE-SpAc](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09983v1)
- MoE-CORE's fastest configuration combines approximate expert substitution with multi-token prediction at depth 2 (TPOT 21.5 ms on DeepSeek GSM8K under an 84 GB cap) — [MoE-CORE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01950)
- Apple silicon, negative: all evaluated speculative-decoding methods reduced throughput in single-request decode across four Apple machines (versus 2-6x claimed on server GPUs) — [Apple Silicon applicability study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31479)
- Apple silicon, SSD-backed: multi-token-prediction speculative decoding was break-even on flash-moe "because MoE I/O scales per token" — [Anemll/flash-moe](https://github.com/Anemll/flash-moe)
- LLM in a Flash (background, dense): speculative decoding gave 1.4x on top of flash loading on an RTX 4090 — [LLM in a flash](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11514v3)
- Early exit: MLX-Flash lists LayerSkip (1.8-2.2x claimed) as "not yet realized" in its own implementation — [MLX-Flash](https://github.com/szibis/MLX-Flash)

### Inferences
- Expected unique experts per layer when verifying n independent rows with K of N active is N x (1 - (1 - K/N)^n). For 8 of 128 and n = 4 that is about 29 experts instead of 8 (3.6x the expert bytes); MoESD's own threshold formula gives about 23 rows to touch 95% of experts at K/N = 1/8. With experts on SSD, each verify step would multiply reads accordingly, so plain speculative decoding is expected to lose on a memory-constrained laptop unless the extra experts are already resident. (Draft rows are correlated, so real unions are somewhat smaller: MoE-Spec measured 54 of 64, not 64, at 127 rows.)
- The remedies that fit a single-GPU 16 GB laptop: (1) Cascade-style utility gating, which is engine-level and exact, guarantees speculation is switched off when it costs more than it gains; (2) MoE-Spec's budget restricted to already-resident experts, which is approximate; (3) MoE-SpAc's idea of using the draft only as a predictor of which experts to prefetch — this gives multi-token lead time for SSD reads without executing extra rows, at the cost of running a draft model in scarce memory.
- Energy note: on unified memory, extra verify rows cost extra matmul rows and extra expert bytes even when wall time is flat, so a speculation policy tuned for time can be energy-negative. No paper measures this directly (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No paper found measures energy per accepted token for speculative decoding on MoE models.
- No study found of speculative decoding for MoE with SSD-resident experts other than the flash-moe anecdote.
- No MoE-specific early-exit / layer-skipping paper from 2025-26 was found in this pass.
- The per-method numbers from the Apple-silicon applicability study were not retrievable (abstract only).

## 5. Serving-system support for MoE as of late 2026

### Takeaway
llama.cpp is the only mainstream engine with user-facing memory-constrained MoE features (`--cpu-moe`, `--n-cpu-moe`, and since October 2026 a merged LRU GPU expert cache, `--moe-cache-mib`); expert-level disk paging exists only as unmerged proofs of concept, the most relevant one being a Metal slot-pool design that ran Qwen3-30B-A3B Q6 on a 16 GB M1 Pro at 13 tok/s. vLLM and SGLang concentrate on multi-GPU expert parallelism and fused kernels, and no evidence was found of expert offloading in upstream mlx-lm, MLC-LLM or TensorRT-LLM; on Apple silicon the capability lives in third-party engines (flash-moe, TurboFieldfare, MLX-Flash, Edge0, ds4).

### Cited Findings

llama.cpp

- `--cpu-moe` keeps all MoE expert weights on the CPU; `--n-cpu-moe N` does so for N layers (env `LLAMA_ARG_N_CPU_MOE`) — [llama-server man page](https://manpages.debian.org/unstable/llama.cpp-tools/llama-server.1.en.html). The man page says "first N layers"; a January 2026 community guide says counting starts from the highest-numbered layers — conflicting descriptions, verify per build — [HF guide by Doctor-Shotgun](https://huggingface.co/blog/Doctor-Shotgun/llamacpp-moe-offload-guide)

### Inferences
- The llama.cpp Metal PoC is the closest public data point to "16 GB unified-memory laptop, model larger than RAM, exact output": 13 tok/s for a roughly 25 GB Q6 model on an M1 Pro 16 GB, and on a 36 GB machine a slot pool of 32 of 128 experts per layer keeps 58% of resident speed (22.1 vs 38.1 tok/s) while cutting wired memory from 27 to 10.6 GiB. Even with every expert resident, the slot indirection costs about 21%.
- Three independent Apple-silicon sources (llama.cpp PoC, ds4 #638, flash-moe's -5x for mmap'd experts) agree that expert weights must not be handed to Metal as mmap-backed no-copy buffers; a fixed pool of explicit, aligned slot buffers filled by positional reads is the working design. This is model-agnostic.
- The merged llama.cpp cache gives a useful sizing rule that should transfer: about 10% of expert bytes resident gives about 72-77% hits and about 30% gives about 89% on a Qwen-family model under LRU, and whether a cache pays depends on the hit rate needed to beat the fallback path (about 85% in one commenter's PCIe model). On unified memory the fallback is SSD rather than CPU compute, so the break-even hit rate is different and must be measured.
- Server-side MoE work (EP, DeepEP, EPLB, DBO) is about inter-GPU communication and load balance; none of it applies to one GPU. The transferable server ideas are fused expert kernels and native 4-bit formats.

### Gaps
- No MoE-specific release notes or documentation were found for MLC-LLM or TensorRT-LLM in this pass (searches returned nothing relevant); their status as of November 2026 is unknown.
- No evidence was found that upstream mlx-lm has expert offloading, lazy expert loading or an expert cache; the mlx-lm repository itself was not inspected directly.
- Whether llama.cpp's merged `--moe-cache-mib` works on the Metal backend is not stated in the PR.
- The follow-up llama.cpp PRs (#26824, #30059, #27861, #25294, RFC #28248) were not opened.

## 6. SSD / flash-based inference for models larger than memory

### Takeaway
The dense-model line (Apple's "LLM in a Flash" and successors) depends on activation sparsity plus predictors and usually ReLU-style retraining, whereas MoE gives the sparsity for free at expert granularity with naturally large reads (1.7-6.75 MB per expert), so 2026 MoE engines get usable speeds from simple designs: one packed file per layer with aligned expert blocks, parallel positional reads from a CPU thread, a resident non-expert core, and either the OS page cache or a per-layer slot cache. Apple-silicon-specific findings recur across projects: mmap'd expert files are much slower and get wired wholesale by Metal, and SSD traffic competes with the GPU for memory bandwidth, so prefetch overlap can be worth nothing or negative.

### Cited Findings

Dense-model flash inference (background and successors)

- LLM in a Flash (Apple, Dec 2023, rev. Jul 2024; background): windowing (keep neurons used by the last 4-5 tokens), row-column bundling (store each FFN up-projection column with its down-projection row to double chunk size), low-rank sparsity predictor (about 5% false negatives, 7% false positives on OPT-6.7B), preallocated per-layer matrices with swap-delete. Flash wants large reads: over 6 GiB/s for a 1 GiB linear read on an M1 Max; 32 KiB or larger random reads across many threads (32 threads in benchmarks) are needed for usable sparse throughput; dense reads about 6.1 GB/s, sparse about 1.25 GB/s, bundled sparse about 2.25 GB/s. With half the model in DRAM: 4.2x (M1 Max Metal), 7.4x (M2 Ultra), about 26x (RTX 4090) over naive loading. Co-activation bundling was a negative result. Measurements bypass the page cache with F_NOCACHE (macOS) / direct I/O (Linux), noting OS caching could help in practice. Models up to 2x DRAM. Needs ReLU-style activation sparsity. **[ARCH]/[FT]** for non-ReLU models — [LLM in a flash](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.11514v3)
- ActiveFlow (Apr 2025, rev. Sept 2025): top-K activation sparsity for modern non-ReLU LLMs via self-distillation (about 10 hours on 4 x A100 per model); current-layer activations predict active weights of the next 4 layers (about 5% loaded on demand); weights reordered so one read fetches the same channel across four layers; io_uring; per-channel unit about 4 KB while peak flash throughput needs chunks above 64 KB. Mixtral-8x7B (24.6 GB) decodes in 2.9 GB at 1.8 tok/s on a Pixel 6. Energy per token down up to 53%, average power -27%. Quality cost is visible (LLaMA-3-8B at 50% sparsity: perplexity 6.97 vs 6.09, GSM8K 38.9 vs 50.9). **[FT]** — [ActiveFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08378v2)
- NeuroPrefetcher (ICPP 2026): one GPU-resident predictor (206.8M parameters, 414 MB) after layer 0 predicts active-neuron masks for all later MLP layers in one pass (about 99% correct centroid choice); only the delta versus the previous token is read (82-85% of rows reused); neuron-centric record layout of 24.5 KiB per neuron, adjacent IDs coalesced into ranges, io_uring with O_DIRECT at queue depth 256 into pinned staging memory. Jetson AGX Orin 32 GB + Samsung 990 PRO: 7.9-12.0x over llama.cpp when the model exceeds memory (1.7-3.7 tok/s vs 0.2-0.3); NVMe I/O is 80-87% of per-token latency; 103 MiB to just under 1 GiB read per token. Dense models only (Mistral-7B, Llama-3-8B). **[P]**, approximate — [NeuroPrefetcher](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.22643.md)
- "VLM in a flash" (Nov 2025): bundling alone is insufficient for vision-language models; predictor-free per-matrix sparsification with neuron chunking, 1.5-3.4x over baseline and 1.7-4.0x over bundling-based implementations (search-snippet level) — [VLM in a flash](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18692)

MoE-specific SSD engines: read sizes, layouts, I/O method

- Expert payload sizes: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B int4 — 1,769,472 bytes (1.6875 MiB = 27 aligned 64 KiB units), 320 experts per forward (40 layers x 8), about 540 MiB if every one misses — [Paging the Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29032v1); same model in Edge0 — about 1.77 MB per expert, about 7 MB per layer at K=4, measured loads averaging 0.27-1.53 MiB — [Edge0](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18063v2); Qwen3.5-397B-A17B — 6.75 MB (4-bit), 5.44 MB (3-bit class), 3.75 MB (2-bit) per expert; 4 experts per layer = 27.0 MB; about 1.62 GB read per token at 4-bit plus about 89 MB dense — [Anemll/flash-moe](https://github.com/Anemll/flash-moe)
- Layouts: one file per layer with expert blocks (gate, up, down projections plus scales and biases together) starting on 64 KiB boundaries (Routide); per-layer stacked safetensors read by byte range (Edge0); one `layer_NN.bin` per layer plus a layout JSON (flash-moe); a repacked single format with experts in kernel-ready binary layout (TurboFieldfare); experts as separate files (FlashMoE). In GGUF the expert index is the outermost storage dimension so each expert is already contiguous (llama.cpp thread) — [Paging the Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29032v1), [Edge0](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18063v2), [Anemll/flash-moe](https://github.com/Anemll/flash-moe), [TurboFieldfare write-up](https://betterstack.com/community/guides/ai/turbo-fieldfare.md), [FlashMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17063v1), [llama.cpp discussion #23324](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23324)
- flash-moe experiment log (M3 Max, SSD measured 17.5 GB/s sequential): parallel `pread` with dispatch groups, each read fanned out over page-aligned chunks (4-way split recommended); expert I/O 0.74 ms per layer (44.7 ms per token) at 4-bit and 0.44 ms per layer (26.7 ms per token) at 3-bit. Kept: dropping the custom Metal LRU cache in favour of the OS page cache (+38%), F_NOCACHE for the 2-bit variant only (+3%). Discarded: mmap'd expert files (-5x), `dispatch_io` (-70%), LZ4-compressed expert cache (-13%), F_RDADVISE prefetch (net 0%), expert file clustering (0%), temporal expert prediction (-18%). Stated cause for prefetch failure: on unified memory SSD DMA and GPU compute share the memory controller — background SSD transfers slowed the GPU by 73%, dequant kernels are bandwidth-saturated at about 418 GiB/s — so the serial GPU-then-SSD-then-GPU pipeline was judged "hardware-optimal" — [danveloper/flash-moe](https://github.com/danveloper/flash-moe), [Anemll/flash-moe](https://github.com/Anemll/flash-moe)
- Opposite design choice that also works: TurboFieldfare has the CPU read the routed experts from SSD while the GPU computes the shared expert, "which hides most of the disk latency", with a 16-expert-per-layer LFU cache (third-party write-up) — [TurboFieldfare write-up](https://betterstack.com/community/guides/ai/turbo-fieldfare.md)
- Edge0 uses mmap + OS page cache + its own LRU with reads overlapped with compute through next-token prediction; prefetching costs about 1.15 GiB of page cache at K=8 and "cold pages arrive in costlier loads"; enlarging the LRU by about 0.9 GiB raised the 8B tier from 28.0 to 31.8 tok/s — [Edge0](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18063v2)
- CPU-only M1 MacBook Air 16 GB, Qwen3-30B-A3B at f16 (larger than RAM), pipelined versus sequential expert I/O as a function of hit rate: 0% — 1.53 vs 1.43 tok/s; 50% — 2.20 vs 2.40; 75% — 2.92 vs 3.81 (1.30x); 88% — 3.47 vs 4.10; 100% — 4.46 vs 4.45 — [llama.cpp discussion #23324](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23324)
- M1 Pro disk tier: parallel reads improved the real-time factor from 25.5 to 16.8 — [llama.cpp issue #20757](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/20757)
- Wired-memory behaviour on macOS: see ds4 issue #638 and the llama.cpp PoC in section 5 — [antirez/ds4 issue #638](https://github.com/antirez/ds4/issues/638)
- Desktop GPU + SSD: FlashMoE loads an expert in about 3 ms from a PCIe 5.0 NVMe; initial load 4x faster than llama.cpp, prefill 2.5x — [FlashMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17063v1); Tideway uses parallel SSD reads on multi-miss and a small streaming window in prefill — [Tideway](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14848v1); on Linux, plain `--mmap` already gives OS-managed paging for llama.cpp, but performance under RAM pressure is poor (0.43 tok/s in the GLM-4.5-Air example; llama.cpp sustains 0.2-0.3 tok/s when a dense model exceeds memory in the NeuroPrefetcher comparison) — [llama.cpp discussion #23324](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/23324), [NeuroPrefetcher](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.22643.md)
- NearSEE (EasyChair preprint, Nov 2026): run routed experts on FPGA computational-storage devices to avoid moving expert weights through host DRAM (Mixtral-8x7B, 32 / 64 GB limits; search-snippet level) — [NearSEE](https://easychair.org/publications/preprint/4fpWj/open)
- iPhone feasibility bounds: sampled process footprint 1.87-2.73 GiB for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, 1.80-3.17 tok/s, a thermal "fair" state stopping one protocol, run-to-run timing differences of about 10% between identically configured runs — [Paging the Experts](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29032v1)

### Inferences
- MoE removes the two hardest parts of "LLM in a Flash": no sparsity predictor is needed for correctness (the router is exact) and reads are already megabyte-sized, far above the 32-64 KiB threshold where flash throughput collapses. Row-column bundling's MoE equivalent — storing an expert's gate, up and down projections contiguously and aligned — is what every 2026 engine does. Co-activation / clustering layouts gave no benefit in either lineage (LLM in a Flash negative result; flash-moe 0%).
- Rough budget for a 16 GB laptop: a 35B-class 4-bit model needs about 540 MiB of expert bytes per token at 8 active of 256 x 40 layers. At a laptop SSD's several GB/s that is on the order of 0.1-0.2 s per token with zero cache hits, so tens of tokens per second require hit rates of roughly 90% or more, or fewer / smaller experts per token (Edge0's K=4, lower bit widths), or both. This is consistent with the published points: 13 tok/s exact at Q6 on a 16 GB M1 Pro (llama.cpp PoC), 6.4 tok/s at K=4 and 3.3 at K=8 on a 16 GB M2 (Edge0), 20 tok/s on 24 GB (Edge0, where page cache covers most reads).
- The evidence on overlapping SSD reads with GPU work on unified memory is mixed and probably depends on expert size: negative for 6.75 MB experts at 1.6 GB per token (flash-moe: GPU slowed 73%), positive for about 1.7 MB experts (TurboFieldfare, Edge0's +80% on 16 GB, 1.09-1.30x CPU-only pipelining at 50-88% hit rates). An engine should measure GPU step time with and without concurrent reads before committing to speculative I/O, and should expect prefetch volume to carry a bandwidth (and energy) cost.
- Page cache versus own cache: with RAM much larger than the working set, trusting the OS cache won (flash-moe: +38%, 71% hits with about 35 GB free). On 16 GB with the GPU needing wired buffers, the working designs keep an explicit fixed pool of slot buffers and read into them directly (llama.cpp PoC, ds4 measurements, TurboFieldfare, Routide), because Metal wires whatever it is handed and wired memory cannot be compressed or swapped.
- Requirements checklist for the 16 GB case, all model-agnostic: resident core; per-layer slot pool above one forward's demand; expert blocks contiguous and aligned (64 KiB or 2 MiB) in a per-layer file; positional parallel reads (not mmap) into slot buffers; router output available to a CPU thread without stalling the GPU timeline; a prefill path that streams whole layers rather than thrashing an LRU (Tideway, MoE-CORE, FlashMoE all treat prefill separately).

### Gaps
- No public head-to-head on Apple silicon of mmap + page cache versus direct / F_NOCACHE positional reads at 16 GB under memory pressure for 1-2 MB experts; flash-moe's numbers are for 48-128 GB machines and 6.75 MB experts.
- Read-queue depth, thread count and alignment sweeps for Apple internal SSDs in the 1-2 MB read regime were not found (LLM in a Flash gives only qualitative curves; flash-moe recommends a 4-way split without a sweep).
- SSD wear / write behaviour is not discussed in any source (reads only), and thermal throttling is reported only anecdotally (iPhone).
- TurboFieldfare details come from a third-party article, not the project's own documentation; Edge0's SSD model and read concurrency are not specified.

## 7. Energy per token for MoE inference and energy-aware routing / scheduling (2025-26)

### Takeaway
Energy work on MoE is thin and mostly server-side, but three results matter for a memory-constrained laptop: flash reads cost about 25x more energy per bit than fast DRAM (about 102-103 pJ/bit versus about 4.2 pJ/bit), so SSD-backed expert loading can dominate per-token energy and prefetching hides latency without removing that energy; on bandwidth-bound edge devices MoE energy follows total rather than active parameters (OLMoE used 2.1x the energy per token of a same-active-size dense model on a Jetson); and the only energy-aware MoE runtimes published tune power caps and batch size, not routing. No paper was found that measures energy per token for MoE on Apple silicon.

### Cited Findings
- "SSD Offloading for LLM MoE Weights Considered Harmful in Energy Efficiency" (IEEE Computer Architecture Letters, Aug 2025): per-bit energy 102.4 pJ/b for flash read + 4.2 pJ/b HBM write + 4.2 pJ/b HBM read = 110.8 pJ/b, about 26x the HBM-only path. Energy per token with experts on SSD versus all-HBM: 4.7-9.8x for DeepSeek-R1 and 3.8-12.5x for Mixtral across batch sizes; 2.1-3.6x more than CPU-DRAM offloading. With SSD offloading, weight access is over 73% (Mixtral) to 80% (DeepSeek-R1) of total energy at batch 1024, versus 11-15% with HBM. Prefetching hides latency (penalty down to 1.25-1.32x) but leaves energy unchanged. A 400B MoE on SSD beats a 70B dense model on HBM only if flash read energy falls to about one tenth (about 10 pJ/b), mainly at batch sizes below 3. Analytical / simulation study with uniform routing; data-center parts — [SSD offloading considered harmful (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06978), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06978)
- SliceMoE's device model uses UFS 3.1 at 103 pJ/bit versus LPDDR4 at up to 1.5 pJ/bit; under it, bit-sliced caching cuts decode energy up to 2.37-2.85x and cache warm-up from prefill statistics cuts decode energy 2.31x versus a cold cache — [SliceMoE](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12990v3)
- "Does MoE Actually Help Inference on Consumer and Edge Hardware?" (June-July 2026): OLMoE-1B-7B versus Llama-3.2-1B (same active parameters) under llama.cpp — about 10% slower on an Apple M2 Pro laptop; on a Jetson Orin Nano 8 GB about 31% slower with 2.1x the energy per token and memory at the device limit. Routing is under 9% of MoE-block compute. Conclusion: on bandwidth-bound edge hardware cost follows total parameters. One model, two devices. Abstract-level — [Empirical study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21428)
- gpt-oss-20b on one H100 (bf16, 2,048-token context, 64 generated tokens; nvidia-smi sampling): 9,764 J per 1,000 generated tokens (about 9.8 J/token) versus 13,155 J (Qwen3-32B dense) and 13,464 J (Yi-34B): 25.8-27.5% lower energy and 31.7-34.6% less VRAM. Energy per 1,000 tokens grows with context (6,673 J at 128 tokens to 9,764 J at 2,048); throughput falls 21.4% from 128 to 2,048 context, more than the dense models — [GPT-OSS-20B deployment analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16700v2)
- Request-versus-token energy (IISWC 2026, CMU; H100 / H200): fixed per-request energy plus marginal per-token energy; for Llama-3.2-1B at batch 16, token energy falls from 7.46 to 0.72 J/token as output grows from 10 to 512 tokens. MoE "amplifies" the fixed cost at low concurrency (sparse routing and fragmented expert execution); batching narrows the dense-versus-MoE token-energy gap. Abstract-level, no MoE figures on the page — [Request and token energy characterization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28044)

### Inferences
- Applying the published flash read energy (about 102 pJ/bit) to a 35B-class model's worst case of about 540 MiB of expert reads per token gives about 0.46 J per token for the reads alone, before the DRAM write and before any compute; at a 90% hit rate that falls to about 0.05 J per token. The per-bit figures come from data-center and UFS parts, not Apple's internal SSD, so this is an order-of-magnitude estimate only. It still implies that on an SSD-backed laptop the cache hit rate is the main energy lever, and that speculative reads which are not used are pure energy loss even when they cost no time.
- Energy-optimal and latency-optimal settings can diverge: prefetching (latency hidden, energy unchanged per the CAL paper), speculative decoding (extra rows and extra expert bytes), and lower-precision-on-miss (fewer bytes read; lower energy and lower latency together) pull in different directions. Byte-reduction techniques (bit-slicing, 3-bit experts, K reduction, pruning) are the ones that improve both.
- "Energy-aware routing" in the sense of changing which experts run to save energy exists only implicitly — as cache-aware routing (section 2) evaluated for energy in SliceMoE's model. Published energy-aware scheduling is about power caps and batch size on servers and does not transfer to a single-user laptop.
- The Jetson result (energy follows total parameters when memory-bandwidth-bound) and the H100 result (gpt-oss-20b about 26% cheaper per token than 32-34B dense models) are compatible: the MoE advantage shows when the full model is resident in fast memory and shrinks or reverses when weight movement dominates.

### Gaps
- No measured joules-per-token for any MoE model on Apple silicon was found, with or without SSD-backed experts; no measurement of Apple internal SSD read energy per bit was found.
- No paper found measures the energy effect of expert caching policy, prefetch accuracy or speculative decoding on a real (non-simulated) memory-constrained device.
- The CAL paper's uniform-routing assumption ignores caching and skew; real per-token SSD energy with a warm cache is unmeasured.
- The MoE-versus-dense numeric tables of the IISWC 2026 characterization were not available from the abstract page.
